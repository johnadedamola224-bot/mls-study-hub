Chapter 1: Introduction to Anatomy

Anatomy is the scientific study of the structure of the human body and the relationships between its parts.

Physiology is the study of how the body and its parts function.

Branches of Anatomy

  • Gross anatomy: Structures visible to the naked eye.
  • Microscopic anatomy: Structures studied using a microscope.
  • Developmental anatomy: Structural changes during growth.
  • Regional anatomy: Structures within a particular body region.
  • Systemic anatomy: Structures grouped by organ system.
  • Clinical anatomy: Application of anatomy to patient care.

Chapter 2: Anatomical Position and Terms

The standard anatomical position is standing upright, facing forward, arms by the sides, palms facing forward, and feet directed forward.

  • Superior: Toward the head.
  • Inferior: Toward the feet.
  • Anterior: Toward the front.
  • Posterior: Toward the back.
  • Medial: Toward the body's midline.
  • Lateral: Away from the midline.
  • Proximal: Nearer the trunk or point of attachment.
  • Distal: Farther from the trunk or point of attachment.
  • Superficial: Near the body surface.
  • Deep: Farther inside the body.

Body Planes

  • Sagittal: Divides the body into right and left portions.
  • Coronal: Divides the body into front and back portions.
  • Transverse: Divides the body into upper and lower portions.

Chapter 3: The Cell

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of the human body.

  • Cell membrane: Controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  • Cytoplasm: Contains organelles and is where many cellular reactions occur.
  • Nucleus: Contains most of the cell's DNA.
  • Mitochondria: Produce much of the cell's usable energy.
  • Ribosomes: Make proteins.
  • Rough endoplasmic reticulum: Helps produce and process proteins.
  • Smooth endoplasmic reticulum: Helps synthesize lipids and process certain substances.
  • Golgi apparatus: Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids.
  • Lysosomes: Contain enzymes that break down materials.

Levels of Organization

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism.

Chapter 4: Basic Tissues

A tissue is a group of similar or related cells working together to perform a function.

  1. Epithelial tissue: Covers surfaces, lines cavities, and forms glands.
  2. Connective tissue: Supports, binds, protects, and transports substances. Examples include bone, cartilage, blood, and adipose tissue.
  3. Muscle tissue: Produces movement. Types are skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle.
  4. Nervous tissue: Receives and transmits signals; it includes neurons and supporting cells.

Chapter 5: The Skeletal System

The adult human skeleton typically contains 206 bones.

Functions

  • Supports the body and gives it shape.
  • Protects internal organs.
  • Works with muscles to produce movement.
  • Stores minerals such as calcium and phosphorus.
  • Bone marrow produces blood cells.

The axial skeleton includes the skull, vertebral column, ribs, and sternum. The appendicular skeleton includes the limbs and their girdles.

Joints are places where bones meet. They may be classified by their structure and the amount of movement they allow.

Chapter 6: The Muscular System

  • Skeletal muscle: Usually under voluntary control and moves the skeleton.
  • Cardiac muscle: Involuntary muscle found in the heart.
  • Smooth muscle: Involuntary muscle found in the walls of many hollow organs and blood vessels.

Muscles help produce movement, maintain posture, stabilize joints, and generate heat.

Chapter 7: Major Organ Systems

  • Cardiovascular: Heart and blood vessels; transports blood.
  • Respiratory: Airways and lungs; exchanges oxygen and carbon dioxide.
  • Digestive: Breaks down food and absorbs nutrients.
  • Urinary: Kidneys, ureters, bladder, and urethra; removes wastes and regulates fluid balance.
  • Nervous: Brain, spinal cord, and nerves; coordinates rapid responses.
  • Endocrine: Glands that release hormones.
  • Lymphatic and immune: Helps defend against infection and returns tissue fluid to circulation.
  • Reproductive: Organs involved in reproduction.
  • Integumentary: Skin and associated structures; protects the body.

Chapter 8: Anatomy in Medical Laboratory Science

Knowledge of anatomy helps Medical Laboratory Science students understand specimen sources, body organs, tissue structure, and the clinical meaning of laboratory findings.

  • Knowledge of blood vessels supports understanding of blood collection.
  • Knowledge of the urinary system helps explain urine formation and collection.
  • Knowledge of organs and tissues helps relate laboratory results to disease processes.
  • Correct anatomical terminology improves communication among healthcare professionals.

Laboratory specimens must be collected, labelled, handled, and processed according to approved procedures.

Chapter 9: Revision Questions

  1. Define anatomy and physiology.
  2. List five branches of anatomy.
  3. Describe the standard anatomical position.
  4. Differentiate between medial and lateral.
  5. Name the four basic tissue types.
  6. State five functions of the skeletal system.
  7. Compare skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle.
  8. List five major organ systems and one function of each.
  9. Explain why anatomy is important in Medical Laboratory Science.
  10. State the levels of biological organization from cell to organism.

Suggested Answers

  1. Anatomy studies body structure; physiology studies body function.
  2. Gross, microscopic, developmental, regional, and systemic anatomy.
  3. Standing upright, facing forward, arms at the sides, palms forward, feet forward.
  4. Medial means nearer the midline; lateral means farther from it.
  5. Epithelial, connective, muscle, and nervous tissue.
  6. Support, protection, movement, mineral storage, and blood-cell production.
  7. Skeletal muscle is usually voluntary; cardiac and smooth muscles are involuntary.
  8. Answers may include cardiovascular transport, respiratory gas exchange, digestion, urinary waste removal, and nervous coordination.
  9. It supports specimen collection, anatomical terminology, and interpretation of laboratory findings.
  10. Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
