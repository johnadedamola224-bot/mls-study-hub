1. Introduction to Biochemistry

Biochemistry is the branch of science that studies the chemical substances, reactions and processes occurring in living organisms. It combines principles of biology and chemistry to explain how cells obtain energy, build structures and maintain life.

Importance of biochemistry in Medical Laboratory Science

  • Helps explain normal body functions and metabolism.
  • Supports laboratory investigation of diseases.
  • Helps interpret substances measured in blood, urine and other samples.
  • Provides a foundation for clinical chemistry and related subjects.

2. Water, pH and Buffers

Water

Water is an important biological solvent. It helps dissolve many substances, transport materials, regulate temperature and support chemical reactions in the body.

pH

pH expresses how acidic or alkaline a solution is. A pH below 7 is acidic, pH 7 is neutral, and pH above 7 is alkaline at approximately 25°C.

Buffers

A buffer resists a large change in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added. Buffer systems help maintain a suitable pH for biological processes.

3. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are organic compounds made mainly of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. They are important sources of energy and also contribute to cell structures and recognition.

Main classes

  • Monosaccharides: simple sugars such as glucose and fructose.
  • Disaccharides: sugars made from two monosaccharide units, such as sucrose and lactose.
  • Polysaccharides: larger carbohydrate chains, such as glycogen, starch and cellulose.

Functions

  • Provide energy for cells.
  • Store energy as glycogen in humans and animals.
  • Participate in cell-surface recognition and biological structures.

4. Proteins and Amino Acids

Proteins are biological molecules made from amino acids joined by peptide bonds. Their structure determines their function.

Functions of proteins

  • Enzymes catalyse biochemical reactions.
  • Antibodies help the immune system recognise foreign substances.
  • Some proteins transport substances, such as oxygen.
  • Structural proteins support cells and tissues.

Protein structure

Protein structure is commonly described at four levels: primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary. Heat, extreme pH and certain chemicals can disrupt protein structure and cause denaturation.

5. Lipids

Lipids are a varied group of substances that are poorly soluble in water. Examples include triglycerides, phospholipids and cholesterol.

Functions

  • Store energy.
  • Form important parts of cell membranes.
  • Provide insulation and protection.
  • Serve as precursors for certain hormones and other molecules.

6. Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of chemical reactions without being consumed in the overall reaction.

Factors affecting enzyme activity

  • Temperature
  • pH
  • Substrate concentration
  • Enzyme concentration
  • Inhibitors and activators

Enzymes are important in laboratory testing. For example, measuring selected enzyme activities can help clinicians investigate tissue injury and disease, interpreted alongside other clinical findings.

7. Introduction to Metabolism

Metabolism refers to all chemical reactions in an organism.

  • Anabolism: reactions that build larger molecules from smaller ones and generally require energy.
  • Catabolism: reactions that break down molecules and often release usable energy.

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is a major energy-carrying molecule used by cells to support many biological processes.

8. Revision Questions

  1. Define biochemistry and state three reasons it is important in Medical Laboratory Science.
  2. What is pH? Distinguish between acidic, neutral and alkaline solutions.
  3. List the three major classes of carbohydrates and give an example of each.
  4. Explain four functions of proteins.
  5. List three functions of lipids.
  6. Define an enzyme and name four factors that affect enzyme activity.
  7. Differentiate between anabolism and catabolism.
  8. What is ATP, and why is it important to cells?
